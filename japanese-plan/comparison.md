# The two plans compared

The **main plan** (this folder) uses Bunpro as the spine. The **Genki plan** ([genki-plan/](genki-plan/README.md)) uses the Genki textbooks and workbooks as the spine. Both take about 430 hours, both end with the same bridge phase and the same tests, and both use the same listening, reading, reference books and Cure Dolly lessons.

## What actually differs

| | Main plan | Genki plan |
|---|---|---|
| What you do with new grammar each day | Read Bunpro's short write-up and example sentences, then copy one sentence and write one of your own | Read Genki's grammar section, answer recorded drills aloud, then fill in workbook pages and mark them from the answer key |
| Feedback on written work | Once a unit, from an assistant | Immediately, from the answer key |
| Spaced review of grammar | Bunpro | Bunpro |
| Vocabulary | Kaishi 1.5k, chosen by frequency, a deck of known quality | Genki's word lists through community-made decks that I have not inspected |
| Graded audio tied to the grammar | None | Dialogues, drills and listening exercises for every lesson |
| Materials open during a session | Anki, Bunpro, a notebook, a video site, a reader | The same, plus a textbook, a workbook, an answer key and an audio app |
| Kana-only pages | None | Textbook Lessons 1–2 and workbook Lessons 1–3 |
| My confidence that sessions fit in two hours | Higher: each block is the same kind of work every day | Lower: lessons vary in size and I have estimated the time from page counts |

## The explanation question

You said you are uncomfortable with grammar being forced into English categories, and that this is why Cure Dolly appeals to you. I want to be accurate about how much the two plans differ on this, because it is less than it appears.

Cure Dolly's course is 33 scheduled lessons, and both plans include all of them. In the main plan you watch them faster, one per session for the first five units, so that her model is in place before most of the grammar arrives. In the Genki plan you watch twelve in the first cycle and then two per cycle.

Outside those lessons, the daily explanation in the main plan is Bunpro's write-up, and Bunpro uses the same conventional terms as Genki, such as る-verbs and う-verbs, and い-adjectives and な-adjectives. Bunpro's write-ups are shorter than Genki's and compare with English less, but they are not a different way of describing the language. I know of no drill-based resource that teaches in Cure Dolly's terms. In both plans her model is something you carry in your head while working from material that uses the standard labels.

So the main plan is better on this point, but only by degree: more of the early explaining is done by Cure Dolly, and the conventional explanations you read are briefer.

## The structure question

Your stated main risk is giving up for lack of structure, and you said that exercises and workbooks are where you see Genki's value.

The Genki plan is stronger here. A workbook page is a fixed task with a right answer that you can check at once, and a lesson is a visible unit of progress through a physical book. The weakest part of the main plan is its notebook block. Copying a sentence is well defined, but writing a sentence of your own depends on you to think of something to say, and you learn whether it was right only at the end of the unit. If any block of the main plan is going to be skipped, it is that one.

The main plan is stronger in a different sense of structure. Every A session is the same, and there are fewer things to have open. The Genki plan asks you to follow a six-session or eight-session sequence in which each session is different, with four more items on the desk.

One further point concerns the main plan. Most experienced learners use Bunpro as a review tool beside a textbook or a grammar guide, not as the thing that teaches them. The main plan uses it as the teacher. I think that works here because Cure Dolly supplies the overall picture and the Dictionary supplies depth, but it is the less tested arrangement of the two.

## My assessment

The two plans are close, and which is better for you depends on one thing I cannot determine from here: how much working from Genki's explanations every day would bother you in practice.

My recommendation is to start with the main plan, for three reasons.

1. It gives Cure Dolly's model the larger role at the stage where the model is formed, which matches what you have told me about how you want grammar explained.
2. It has fewer parts, and I am more confident that its sessions fit in two hours.
3. You already like Bunpro, and a tool you like is one you are more likely to open every evening.

I would not hold that recommendation strongly. Earlier today I recommended the Genki plan, before you told me how much weight you put on the explanation style. The Genki plan is the better answer to the worry you named first, and it is a sound plan.

Two things would settle it better than my judgement.

**A trial before you start.** You have the Genki I PDFs. Spend one evening on Lesson 4, which is the first lesson printed in normal orthography in both the textbook and the workbook: read its grammar section and do three workbook pages, marking them from the answer key. If the explanations read as tolerable and the pages feel satisfying, choose the Genki plan. If the explanations grate, choose the main plan.

**A switch point if you start with the main plan.** At Test 1, after Unit 10, look at your session log. If the notebook block was skipped or cut short in more than a quarter of the sessions, or if you scored below 75% on grammar, move to the Genki plan at cycle 12, the start of Genki II. Bunpro's N5 lessons cover nearly the same ground as Genki I, so little is lost. You would need to add the "Genki II" Bunpro deck, begin the Genki II vocabulary deck in place of new Kaishi cards, and read through the Genki I grammar sections once to check for gaps.
