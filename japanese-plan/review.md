# Review brief

Paste the text below into a new session started in this folder.

---

Please review the Japanese study plans in this folder. They were written by another Claude session for me, and I want an independent check before I commit several hundred hours to one of them. Do not edit any of the files. Report your findings to me.

## What is here

- `prompt.md` states my situation, my constraints and what I asked for. Treat it as the specification.
- `README.md`, `resources.md`, `sessions.md` and `schedule.md` are the main plan, which uses Bunpro as its spine.
- `genki-plan/` is the alternative plan, which uses the Genki textbooks and workbooks as its spine. It refers to the main plan's files for the blocks the two plans share.
- `comparison.md` compares the two plans and recommends one.
- `gemini-review.md` records where the author disagreed with earlier advice I had from Gemini. It is background and does not need reviewing, except where the plans rely on a claim made in it.

The folder `../downloads/` contains material you can check claims against. The PDFs of the Genki I textbook, workbook and answer key (third edition) are `Genki-vol1-ed3.pdf`, `Genki-vol1-ed3-workbook.pdf` and `Genki-vol1-ed3-answer-key.pdf`. The folder `../downloads/website-content/` holds the saved Gemini conversations in `gemini/` and the saved listing pages from Natural Japanese (`nijapanese.com.html`) and Nihongo no Jikan (`nihongo-jikan.com.html`).

## Order of work

Your memory may contain a note written by the session that produced these plans. It summarises that session's conclusions, including which plan it recommends. If it is there, ignore that note, and any other memory entry about these plans, so that your review rests only on the files and on what you verify yourself.

Read `prompt.md` first, then the two plans. Form your own view of which plan suits me better before you read `comparison.md`, and then say whether you agree with it and why.

## What to check

1. **Fit to the specification.** Does each plan do what `prompt.md` asks? Note anything asked for that is missing and anything included that I did not ask for and that does not earn its place.

2. **Time.** Add up the minutes in every session layout. Then test whether the work assigned to each block can be done in its minutes: the daily Anki load at the stated number of new cards, the number of Bunpro points per session, the time for a Cure Dolly lesson, the time for a Genki lesson including its workbook pages, and the Shin Kanzen Master work in the final phase. Check the totals for sessions, hours and weeks, and the calendar dates.

3. **Facts.** Verify the factual claims about each resource against primary sources: publishers' pages, the services' own sites and the PDFs in `../downloads/`. Weigh the quality of anything else you find. The author marked some claims as unverified or as written from memory, and these deserve the most attention:
   - the section titles of the introduction to *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*, and the statement that its examples have romaji and no furigana;
   - the grammar lists for Genki II, Lessons 13 to 23;
   - the internal structure of the two Shin Kanzen Master N4 books and the time allowed per lesson;
   - the two community-made Genki vocabulary decks on AnkiWeb, which were not inspected;
   - the contents of the Tokini Andy membership, which were taken from its sales page;
   - the names of Bunpro's settings and the item counts of its Genki decks;
   - the Cure Dolly lesson numbers, which follow a community transcript and may not match the numbering on YouTube;
   - the choice of first series on Satori Reader.

4. **Consistency.** The files were edited many times. Look for numbers, names, unit or cycle references and cross-references that disagree between files or within one file.

5. **Clarity of instruction.** I need to be told exactly what to do. Flag every instruction that leaves me to decide something, depends on a judgement I may not be able to make as a beginner, or could be read in two ways.

6. **Soundness.** Say where you would have designed either plan differently, and separate real problems from differences of taste. In particular: is the number of resources in use at one time manageable, is the balance between flashcard review and reading and listening reasonable, and are the three tests and their pass marks sensible gates?

7. **The comparison.** Is `comparison.md` fair to both plans? Is its recommendation supported by what it says, and are the trial and the switch point it proposes workable?

8. **Prose.** I asked for clear, literal sentences without slogans, fragments or unexplained shorthand, and without padding or repetition. Point out passages that fail this.

## How to report

- List findings in order of importance. For each one give the file, the place in it, what is wrong, and the change you would make.
- Keep errors of fact or arithmetic separate from matters of judgement.
- Say what you checked and found correct, and say what you could not check.
- If something in the plans or in this brief is unclear, ask me before assuming.
- Access websites sparingly. Do not use my logged-in browser sessions.
