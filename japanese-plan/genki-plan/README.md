# Alternative plan: Genki as the spine

This is a complete alternative to the main plan in the parent folder. It has the same goal, the same two hours a day and the same end point, which is readiness for Shin Kanzen Master N3. The difference is what sets the order of grammar and supplies the written exercises. In the main plan that is Bunpro and a notebook. Here it is the Genki textbooks and workbooks, with Bunpro kept only as the review system.

[comparison.md](../comparison.md) in the parent folder compares the two plans and gives my recommendation.

## The plan at a glance

The unit of this plan is the **lesson cycle**: the sessions spent on one Genki lesson. A cycle has several A sessions, which work through the lesson, and one B session, which consolidates it.

| Phase | Content | Cycles | Sessions per cycle | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Genki I | Lessons 1–12 (Lessons 1 and 2 share one cycle) | 11 | 7 (6 A + 1 B) | Official JLPT N5 practice test |
| 2. Genki II | Lessons 13–23 | 11 | 9 (8 A + 1 B) | Official JLPT N4 practice test (2012) |
| 3. Bridge | *Shin Kanzen Master N4 Grammar* and *Reading*; the N5 and N4 Bunpro points that Genki does not cover | 6 units | 6 (5 A + 1 B) | Official JLPT N4 practice test (2018) |

That is 215 sessions including the three tests, or about 430 hours, which is the same as the main plan. At six sessions a week it takes 36 weeks and at five it takes 43.

### The vocabulary deck

Genki's exercises assume you know the lesson's vocabulary, so vocabulary has to follow the book's order. Kaishi 1.5k is therefore not used in this plan. Genki I and II teach about 1,700 words between them, which is slightly more than Kaishi.

AnkiWeb has community-made decks for the third edition. The two I would start from are "Genki 1 3rd edition with sound files" (about 1,076 notes, the most used, with a notice from its author that it may not stay available) and "Genki 2 Vocabulary with Audio (3rd Edition)". I have not inspected either. On the setup evening, download both, check that they are divided by lesson, and check that the front of a card shows the word in kanji. If the front shows kana or English, change the card template so that it shows the kanji form.

The alternative is Bunpro's own deck "Genki I 3rd Edition", which contains 698 vocabulary items as well as the grammar, in lesson order. It is official and maintained, and it would put grammar and vocabulary in one review queue. I prefer Anki here because its card format is known to suit an RTK graduate and because decks exist for both volumes, but if the AnkiWeb decks turn out to be poor, use Bunpro's.

## Tokini Andy

Tokini Andy is run by Andy and Yuki. It has two parts.

**Free on YouTube.** There is a grammar video for every Genki lesson. Forum posts describe two versions: an edited one of about 30 minutes and the original live stream of about 90 minutes. Each video goes through the lesson's grammar points with many example sentences. Learners on the WaniKani forum who studied Genki alone consistently describe these videos as a large help. The framing of the grammar is the same conventional one that Genki uses.

**The membership.** It costs $10 a month or $100 a year. The site's own description lists the following, and I could not see inside the members' area:

- A course page for each Genki lesson with a checklist that you tick off, and a progress display for each course.
- Member-only versions of the Genki grammar videos, and "bonus sentence" grammar videos.
- Textbook practice videos, in which the exercises of the textbook's Practice section are worked through.
- Vocabulary videos, and listening and shadowing videos.
- A test for each Genki lesson.
- Speaking and writing challenges on which Andy and Yuki give feedback, and a members' forum where they answer questions.
- More than 100 graded stories with native audio, described as an "immersion material course".
- The same for the intermediate textbook Quartet 1 and 2, which would matter after this plan.
- A flashcard web app with N5 and N4 kanji and vocabulary decks, and a kanji course.

For someone working through Genki alone, three of these are worth money. The textbook practice videos replace the part of Genki that this plan otherwise has to skip, because the Practice section is written for pairs. The lesson tests give a checkpoint at the end of every cycle. The writing challenges give feedback from a person. The flashcard app and kanji course duplicate Anki and RTK, and you should not use them.

My advice is to use the free grammar videos from the first cycle, and to take the membership for one month when you reach Lesson 3 or 4. Keep it if the practice videos and tests are good and fit the time available. [sessions.md](sessions.md) says where each piece goes. The plan works without the membership.

## How many explanations to read

A risk in this plan is that every grammar point has five possible explanations: Genki's, Tokini Andy's, Bunpro's, Cure Dolly's and the Dictionary's. Reading all five would take the whole session. The plan assigns each a single role:

- **Genki's grammar section** is the explanation you study for every point, because the exercises refer to it.
- **Tokini Andy's free video** may be watched before reading Genki's section, in which case the reading becomes a quick review.
- **Bunpro's write-up** is read only when you get a review wrong.
- **Cure Dolly** is a separate short course on how Japanese sentences work, watched in its own order and not matched to Genki lessons until Phase 2.
- **The Dictionary** is consulted in the B session for points you got wrong in the workbook.

## Known weaknesses of this plan

- Genki's explanations use the conventional English-based framing, which is the thing you said you are uncomfortable with. Cure Dolly runs alongside to give you the other view, but the book you work from every day will use terms such as "ru-verb" and "u-verb".
- About half of each Practice section is pair or group work, which you cannot do alone.
- Textbook Lessons 1 and 2 and workbook Lessons 1 to 3 are printed in kana with spaces. Write your own answers in kanji from the start.
- There are more things to handle at once than in the main plan: a textbook, a workbook, an answer key and an audio app, in addition to Anki, Bunpro, the videos and the reader.
- My estimate of the time a lesson takes is based on the page counts of the books and the publisher's guideline of six class hours per lesson. It has not been tested on you.
