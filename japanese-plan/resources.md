## Your other subscriptions

This plan does not use Migaku, Skritter or Kanshudo, and you could pause them.

- **Migaku** is a tool for collecting flashcards from native video and web pages. That is the right activity for the N3 stage and later, when anime comes into your study. For the small amount of word collecting in Units 26–36, Yomitan does the same job, though you could use Migaku for it instead since you already know it.
- **Skritter** trains handwriting of characters and words. Your RTK reviews already maintain character writing, and the notebook block has you write whole sentences, which is the more useful practice at this stage.
- **Kanshudo** is mainly a kanji and vocabulary study system. Both of those jobs are already assigned to RTK and Anki.

## The core resources

### Bunpro

Bunpro is a website that teaches grammar points in a fixed order and then tests them with spaced repetition. A test shows a Japanese sentence with a gap and you type the missing particle or conjugated form. By my count of its public list, N5 has 132 points in 10 lessons and N4 has 185 points in 10 lessons. Each point has a written explanation, example sentences with audio (5 on a free account, 12 on a paid one), and a list of outside references, which includes the page numbers in *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar* and usually a Cure Dolly video.

Weaknesses: some points are split very finely (これ, それ and あれ are three separate points), and the gap-fill format occasionally leaves it unclear which of two similar answers it wants. It gives a hint when this happens.

Settings to choose on the setup evening (Bunpro renames settings from time to time, so match by meaning):

- Follow the standard JLPT order, starting with N5 Lesson 1. Do not choose one of the textbook-based orders.
- Use the review type in which you type the answer, not the one in which you only read and grade yourself.
- Hide furigana if the setting lets you reveal the reading of a word by clicking it. Otherwise leave furigana on.
- Leave Bunpro's vocabulary decks switched off.

### Cure Dolly, "Japanese from Scratch"

This is a series of YouTube videos on the channel "Organic Japanese with Cure Dolly". The author died in 2021 and the videos remain available. The value of the series is its model of the sentence: every sentence has a predicate, which is a verb, an い-adjective or a noun with だ, everything else attaches to that predicate, and conjugation is a regular process of attaching helper words to one of a few stems. This is the explanation that avoids forcing Japanese into English grammar, which is what you asked for.

A complete transcript with a table of contents is at <https://khanhduy743.github.io/cure-dolly-transcript>. The lesson numbers in [schedule.md](schedule.md) follow that table of contents. Lessons "7.5" and "8b" are separate videos, and their titles are "Japanese conjugation made easy" and "Particles explained".

Weaknesses you should know about:

- The synthetic voice and audio quality put many people off. Turn on the subtitles. If that is not enough, read the transcript instead of watching.
- After about lesson 35 the series stops being a course and becomes a collection of individual topics. This plan schedules lessons 1–33 and a handful of later ones.
- She presents her analysis as the truth that textbooks hide. Most of it is a restatement of how Japanese school grammar and writers such as Jay Rubin describe the language. A few points are one side of a real disagreement among linguists and are stated as settled. The table below lists the ones that matter.

Her terms against the terms used by Bunpro and *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*:

| Cure Dolly | Bunpro and the Dictionary | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| "A car" and "B engine" | subject and predicate | The same thing. |
| zero pronoun, invisible が | ellipsis, omitted subject | The same observation. The Dictionary covers it under "Ellipsis". |
| logical particles (が, を, に, へ, で) | case particles | The same thing. |
| non-logical particles (は, も) | topic marker, topic particle | The same thing. |
| adjective | い-adjective | |
| adjectival noun | な-adjective | |
| い-stem | ます-stem, Vmasu | |
| あ-stem + ない | negative form | |
| helper verb, helper adjective | auxiliary | |
| receptive helper verb れる/られる | passive form | She says the subject "receives" the action and that this is not a passive. Standard descriptions call it the passive. The sentences are identical. |
| In りんごが好きだ, 日本語が分かる and 水が飲みたい, the thing marked by が is the subject | The Dictionary says が marks the object of certain stative predicates | These are two analyses of the same sentences. Her version is the easier one to produce correct sentences from. When the Dictionary says "object marked by が", it is describing the same が. |

### A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar

This is the standard English-language reference for N5 and N4 grammar. It has about 200 alphabetical entries, each with a formation rule, key sentences, further examples, and notes that compare the item with similar ones. Before the entries there is a glossary of grammatical terms and an essay of about 45 pages called "Characteristics of Japanese Grammar", and after them there are appendices with conjugation tables and lists of transitive and intransitive verb pairs.

Example sentences are printed in normal Japanese script without furigana, followed by a romaji line and an English translation. Cover the romaji line with a card while you read the Japanese.

The plan uses the book in two fixed ways. In Phase 1 you read one section of the introductory essay in each B session. In every B session of Phases 1 and 2 you look up the grammar points that Bunpro shows you got wrong most often that week.

### Kaishi 1.5k

Kaishi 1.5k is a free Anki deck of about 1,500 common words, selected by frequency from several older decks. The front of a card shows the word in kanji and an example sentence. The back gives the reading, the meaning, a translation of the sentence, and audio for the word and the sentence. Download the deck from the releases page of <https://github.com/donkuri/Kaishi>.

This format suits an RTK graduate because the kanji on the front are already familiar shapes with a rough meaning, so each card only asks you to learn how the word sounds and what exactly it means.

The deck has pitch accent data that is switched off by default. Leave it off. Imitate the audio when you say each word, and take up pitch accent with a tutor at the N3 stage if you want to.

### Natural Japanese

Natural Japanese, formerly Comprehensible Japanese, is a library of videos in which a teacher talks in slow Japanese about a topic while drawing or showing pictures. Videos are graded Complete Beginner, Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced, and they have Japanese transcripts. Nothing is explained in English. The drawings are what let you follow speech that you could not follow as audio alone.

The site changed hands in June 2026. Ben Maerkle, who had been the business partner of the founder and original presenter, Yuki, since 2023, announced on the site's forum on 5 June 2026 that the partnership was ending, that he had bought her half of the company and would continue the site under the new name, and that she was starting a new project. No further reason has been published. Yuki now publishes at <https://nihongo-jikan.com> and on YouTube, free to watch and funded by donations.

Her videos are being removed from Natural Japanese in equal monthly portions over two years, ending in mid-2028, and the site has a "Videos leaving soon" filter. This does not threaten the plan. The site's catalogue on 15 November 2026 lists the following, where "by others" excludes every video Yuki made:

| Complete Beginner | 404 | 63 | 180 | 25 |

The other teachers have published about 30 videos a month between them throughout 2026, so these numbers are growing. The plan needs about 8 hours at Complete Beginner and about 35 hours at Beginner and above, so the library is sufficient even with none of Yuki's videos. Nihongo no Jikan has 348 Beginner videos totalling 75 hours.

Use Natural Japanese for the listening block. Hearing a dozen speakers is better practice than hearing one, and each video carries a difficulty number that the site can sort by, which the listening block uses. Nihongo no Jikan is an equivalent free substitute if you ever stop subscribing.

Users rate the other teachers about as highly as Yuki. In a sample of 111 Complete Beginner and Beginner videos published between June 2025 and May 2026, the median number of likes per video was 66 for Yuki's and 72 for the others', with every teacher's typical video falling between about 55 and 80. Forum comments single out Yuki's early whiteboard videos as the easiest starting point for a new learner. Those are the videos to watch on her own site if the easiest Complete Beginner videos on Natural Japanese feel too hard.

### Tadoku free graded readers

The non-profit Tadoku publishes free short illustrated books at <https://tadoku.org/japanese/en/free-books-en/>, graded Start, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Level 1 corresponds to N5 and Level 2 to N4. Most books use kanji with furigana and many have audio. A few of the lowest-level books are marked ひらがなのみ (hiragana only), and you can skip those.

### Satori Reader

Satori Reader is a library of serialised stories and dialogues written for learners. Every sentence has audio by a voice actor, a translation, and notes written by the editors on vocabulary and grammar. In the settings you can choose to show all words in kanji and to reveal furigana only when you tap a word. The site sorts its series into Easier, Intermediate and Harder.

The site describes itself as for intermediate learners. With N5 grammar complete and about 600 words, an Easier series is slow but workable because of the translations and notes. Start with "Human Japanese Extra Credit", which is in the Easier group and accompanies the same company's beginner course, and then take other Easier series in whatever order interests you. Where a series has an easier and a harder edition, choose the easier one.

### The official JLPT practice workbooks

The JLPT organisers publish two practice workbooks for each level, one from 2012 and one from 2018, as free PDFs with audio, scripts and answer keys at <https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/sampleindex.html>. Each has about as many questions as a real test. The plan uses the 2012 N5 paper, the 2012 N4 paper and the 2018 N4 paper as tests, and keeps the 2018 N5 paper as a spare. The N5 and N4 papers are written largely in kana, which you dislike, but each is a single two-hour measurement.

## The Kanji Code

*The Kanji Code* catalogues the phonetic components of kanji. A phonetic component is a part of a character that indicates its on-reading, as 青 does in 清, 晴, 精 and 静, which are all read セイ. Because RTK taught you to see characters as assemblies of components, this is a natural extension of what you already know, and it helps with guessing and remembering the readings of words made of two or more kanji.

Its value depends on timing. Before you know several hundred words there is nothing for the patterns to attach to. After about 1,500 words you will have noticed many of the patterns yourself. That is why I suggest Units 21–24 if you buy it. It is a short book that explains one useful idea, and the plan does not depend on it.

## Nihongo con Teppei for Beginners

This podcast has well over a thousand episodes of about four minutes, all in Japanese. It is audio only and unscripted, and transcripts are available only to its Patreon supporters, so it is hard to follow before you have most of N5. The plan does not schedule it. From about Unit 8 it is good extra listening for time outside your sessions, such as a walk or a commute.
