Claude Anime recommendations prompting
======================================

When I ran the prompt below, Claude asked if it could recommend a few movies. As it turned out, I'd watched all it's recommendations. But for next time, add in ranked list of movies so it can get some input from that as well.

On the basis of its current recommendations, I downloaded Pluto, ODDTAXI and Summer Time Rendering.

Prompt
------

These are anime series that I've watched ranked from favorite to least favorite:

* Erased
* Steins;Gate
* Terminator Zero
* Frieren
* Attack on Titan
* Psycho Pass
* Cowboy Bebop
* Dandadan
* Neon Genesis Evangelion
* Chainsaw Man
* Monster
* Demon Slayer

I didn't like Demon Slayer at all, I just feel it's aimed at a younger audience (I'm 56). I feel I should have liked Monster much more, as I prefer series with an episode spanning story arc rather than highly episodic content like Cowboy Bebop (where you could almost watch it in any order). But I just never found Tenma sympathetic, in fact, I found him rather tiresome (everything else is great). Cowboy Bebop is rescued by its sheer style. I found the main protagonist in Steins;Gate also rather sympathetic, but grew to like him (tho' he then blew it for me in the epilogue). I did like Frieren, but despite the major story arc, it is still quite episodic in nature. I found Dandadan charming even if there's not really much of a story, though I liked the second season less. Neon Genesis Evangelion is interesting, but it is a weird mix of kid's giant robot stuff with something darker and more complex. Maybe I should have ranked Chainsaw Man, higher, but there's no great depth to it. I'm not sure whether to class Terminator Zero as an anime - it is in Japanese, but it feels like American's making an anime, but I did like the fact it had a coherent story from start to finish. I loved Erased, even if you could see it coming that the lovely teacher was being set up to be the monster as a rather too deliberate shocker.

OK - given the ranking and my comments, recommend 10 or so major anime from the last forty years that you think I'd like (with a slight bias towards modern rather than very old content).

Feel free to ask me questions if you think it would improve your answer.

Answer
------

Ranked by how well I think each fits your taste, best first. All are complete stories unless noted.

1. **Pluto** (2023, 8 eps). Naoki Urasawa again, the Monster author, but this time the lead is a robot detective you'll actually root for. Serialized murder mystery with a closed ending.
2. **Odd Taxi** (2021, 13 eps). A middle-aged taxi driver is drawn into a missing-girl case. Ignore the animal-headed character designs; it's a tight, adult mystery where every thread pays off in the finale.
3. **Summertime Render** (2022, 25 eps). Time-loop murder mystery on a small island. The closest thing to Erased crossed with Steins;Gate, and it lands its ending. Some fan service in the early episodes.
4. **From the New World** (2012, 25 eps). Dark far-future sci-fi following one group from childhood to adulthood, adapted from a single novel so the plotting is airtight. It has a couple of odd tonal detours mid-run; push through them.
5. **Showa Genroku Rakugo Shinju** (2016 to 2017, 25 eps). Decades-spanning drama about two rakugo performers, a love triangle, and a lifetime of guilt. The most grown-up anime I can name.
6. **Vinland Saga** (2019 to 2023, 48 eps so far). Viking epic. Season one is a revenge thriller, season two becomes a slow character study that many consider the better half. Not finished: the anime hasn't reached the manga's ending yet.
7. **Cyberpunk: Edgerunners** (2022, 10 eps). Studio Trigger's tight, tragic arc with Cowboy Bebop levels of style. Very violent and explicit. Like Terminator Zero it's a Western property, but this one feels unmistakably Japanese.
8. **Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood** (2009 to 2010, 64 eps). The most satisfying start-to-finish arc in mainstream anime, with war, genocide and grief at its core. It has shonen roots and some chibi comedy early on that may remind you of Demon Slayer; it grows out of them.
9. **Death Note** (2006 to 2007, 37 eps). Serialized cat-and-mouse thriller. The protagonist is a villain by design rather than someone you're asked to like, and the detective opposing him carries the show. Consensus is that it peaks about two-thirds in.
10. **Kids on the Slope** (2012, 12 eps). Watanabe, the Bebop director, on 1960s Kyushu, jazz and friendship. Short, warm and coherent, with a Yoko Kanno score.

Also consider: **Parasyte: The Maxim** (2014, 24 eps, sympathetic lead, complete), **Heavenly Delusion** (2023, 13 eps, excellent but the story is only partway through), and **Legend of the Galactic Heroes** (1988 to 1997, 110 eps) if you ever want an old serialized space opera with an entirely adult cast.

Films: **Perfect Blue** (1997), a psychological thriller squarely in your lane. **Ghost in the Shell** (1995), the direct ancestor of Psycho-Pass. **Princess Mononoke** (1997), Miyazaki at his least child-oriented.
