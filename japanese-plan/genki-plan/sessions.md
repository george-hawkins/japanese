# Sessions (Genki plan)

Every session is two hours. An A session works through part of a Genki lesson. A B session closes the lesson. A T session is a test.

Where this file says "as in the main plan", the instructions are in the main plan's [sessions.md](../sessions.md).

## How to do each block

### Anki

Do your RTK reviews, then the reviews of the Genki vocabulary deck, then the new cards. Grade the cards as in the main plan.

Use the same deck options as in the main plan, except for the number of new cards. At the start of each cycle, unsuspend or enable the cards for that lesson and set new cards per day to the number of words in the lesson divided by the number of A sessions in the cycle, rounded up. This is usually between 9 and 13. The whole lesson's vocabulary is then introduced by the last A session, and the first half of it by the time you start the workbook.

Many words in Genki I will be ones you remember. Press Easy on those.

In Phase 3 there is no more Genki vocabulary. Collect up to ten words per session from Satori Reader with Yomitan, as in the main plan.

### Bunpro reviews

As in the main plan. In this plan you never use Bunpro to learn a point for the first time. After you have studied a grammar point in Genki, add the matching item from the Genki deck to your reviews. This takes a minute or two per point and is part of the Genki block.

In Phase 3 the block is 20 minutes, and after your reviews you add up to four new points per session from the N5 and N4 lists that the Genki decks did not include. There are about 130 of these. Many are small variants of points you already know, and for these, Bunpro's write-up is the first explanation you read.

### Genki: how to work through a lesson

A lesson has a dialogue, a vocabulary list, a grammar section with five to eight numbered points, expression notes, a Practice section, and a set of workbook pages. The workbook has one page for each grammar point, then a Questions page and a Listening Comprehension page. You do not use the Reading and Writing section at the back of either book, because it is the book's kanji course.

The table gives the Genki block for each A session of a Phase 1 cycle, which has six A sessions.

| Session | What to do in the Genki block |
|---|---|
| A1 | **Dialogue and first grammar points.** Listen to the dialogue twice with the book closed. Read it, then read it aloud along with the recording. Read the first half of the grammar points. Add those points in Bunpro. |
| A2 | **Remaining grammar.** Watch the scheduled Cure Dolly lesson. Read the rest of the grammar points and the expression notes. Add those points in Bunpro. |
| A3 | **Practice, points 1 and 2 (and 3 if there are seven or more).** For each point, do the recorded drills in the Practice section and then the workbook page. |
| A4 | **Practice, the next two points.** Watch the scheduled Cure Dolly lesson. Then drills and workbook pages as in A3. |
| A5 | **Practice, the remaining points.** Drills and workbook pages. |
| A6 | **Closing pages.** Do the workbook's Questions page and Listening Comprehension page. Listen to the dialogue once more and read it aloud without the recording. |

A Phase 2 cycle has eight A sessions and a 40-minute block. Use A1 and A2 for the dialogue and grammar, A3 to A7 for practice at one or two points per session, and A8 for the closing pages. Cure Dolly lessons go in A2, A4 and A6.

**Recorded drills.** In the Practice section, exercises marked with a speaker symbol have recorded answers. Look at the cue, say your answer aloud, then play the recording and repeat the correct answer. Skip every exercise labelled Pair Work, Group Work, Class Activity or Role Play.

**Workbook pages.** Write every answer by hand in full kanji, including in the early lessons where the workbook itself is printed in kana. Mark each page from the answer key as soon as you finish it, and put a cross beside every wrong answer. A page should take 10 to 15 minutes.

**If you use Tokini Andy.** The free grammar video for the lesson can replace the first reading of the grammar section in A1 and A2. Watch the edited version, not the live stream. Afterwards, skim Genki's own grammar section, which will take about ten minutes. If you take the membership, its textbook practice videos replace the recorded drills in A3 to A5, and its lesson test goes at the start of the Corrections block in the B session.

**If the lesson does not fit.** The rules of the main plan apply: reviews are always finished, listening and reading always get their time, and the Genki block is what gives way. A cycle may therefore take an extra A session. If the first three cycles of Genki II each need two extra sessions, treat ten A sessions as the normal length from then on.

### Corrections (B sessions)

Redo, by hand and without looking at your earlier attempt, every workbook answer you marked wrong during the cycle. For any that you get wrong again, read the entry in *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*, using the page reference that Bunpro gives for that point, and copy the entry's key sentences into your notebook.

### Dictionary introduction and Book (B sessions)

As in the main plan: one section of the Dictionary's introduction per B session in Phase 1, followed by a short written summary from memory, and about 13 pages of *Making Sense of Japanese* per B session in Phase 2.

### Composition (B sessions)

As in the main plan. Use at least three grammar points from the lesson you have just finished. If you are a Tokini Andy member, you can submit the text to its writing challenge for feedback from a person in addition to, or in place of, the check by an assistant.

## Rules

The rules of the main plan for long reviews, days off and missed days apply unchanged. Two points are specific to this plan:

- Do not start the workbook pages of a lesson before you have read its grammar section, even if you remember the grammar from earlier study.
- Do not do the Reading and Writing section of either book, and do not add its kanji exercises. Your RTK reviews cover that ground.
