# Japanese study plan: N5 and N4, up to the start of Shin Kanzen Master N3

This plan covers two hours a day, five or six days a week, from a restart at N5 to the point where you can open the Shin Kanzen Master N3 books. It is written for someone who has completed RTK1 and still reviews it, reads kana fluently, half-remembers N5, wants normal kanji orthography from the first day, and wants to be told exactly what to do in each session.

## The plan at a glance

The plan is divided into 36 units. A unit is six sessions of two hours: five sessions that introduce new material (A sessions) and one that consolidates (a B session). At six sessions a week a unit takes one week; at five sessions a week it takes a little over one week. The whole plan is about 430 hours of study.

If you start on Monday 16 November 2026 and take no breaks, Phase 1 ends between late January and early February 2027, Phase 2 between mid-June and late July 2027, and Phase 3 between late July and mid-September 2027. The earlier date in each pair assumes six sessions a week and the later one five. Holidays and illness will add to this, so expect to reach Shin Kanzen Master N3 in the autumn of 2027.

## How the plan supplies structure

Your main worry was giving up for lack of structure, so the plan is built around five things that a textbook course normally provides.

1. Every session has the same blocks in the same order, each with a minute budget. You never have to decide what to do next.
2. The schedule says exactly which grammar points, videos, book sections and reading level belong to each unit.
3. Progress is counted in sessions, not calendar weeks. A missed evening moves the end date back by one evening and has no other effect, so you cannot be "behind".
4. There are written rules for what to do when reviews take too long or when you have missed several days.
5. There are three timed tests with pass marks, so you know objectively whether to move on.

## The main design decisions

**Bunpro replaces the textbook.** It has a fixed order of grammar points, a written explanation and example sentences for each, and daily typed exercises that it schedules for you. Its sentences are written in normal kanji orthography. For each grammar point it lists the matching pages of *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*, and for many it also lists the matching Cure Dolly video.

**Cure Dolly is watched early and in her own order.** Her course builds each lesson on the previous ones, so it does not work when rearranged to follow Bunpro. You watch lessons 1–33 during Phase 1, which means you meet some N4 structures for the first time there, and you re-watch the relevant lessons when Bunpro reaches those structures in Phase 2.

**There is one flashcard system for vocabulary.** All vocabulary goes through Anki: first Kaishi 1.5k, then words you collect while reading. Bunpro's vocabulary features and Satori Reader's built-in flashcards stay switched off, because two or three separate review queues for the same kind of item is the most common way for a plan like this to become unmanageable.

**Kanji readings are learned through words.** You do not study on-readings and kun-readings as lists. Each Kaishi card shows a word in kanji that you already recognise from RTK, and you learn how that word is pronounced. The readings accumulate from the words.

**Listening starts with video, not podcasts.** At N5, a teacher drawing on a whiteboard while speaking slow Japanese is understandable. An audio-only podcast mostly is not. I chose Natural Japanese on that basis and not because you already pay for it.

**Reading starts with Tadoku and moves to Satori Reader.** Tadoku's free books are readable from the first week. Satori Reader assumes you already have basic grammar, so it starts in Phase 2. Satori Reader lets you display every word in kanji with the reading hidden until you tap, which is the display you want.

**Handwriting has a daily place and speaking is solo for now.** A notebook block in every session has you copy and compose sentences by hand in full kanji. Speaking consists of reading aloud and repeating after audio. A tutor is left for the N3 stage, as you asked.

**The last six units are a workbook phase.** *Shin Kanzen Master N4 Grammar* and *Reading* give you the pen-and-paper exercises you liked about the Genki route, in normal orthography, and in the same format as the N3 books you are heading for.

## Assumptions to check

- Your daily RTK reviews happen inside the 30-minute Anki block. I have assumed they take about five minutes. If they take much longer, say so and I will rebalance.
- You do the sessions at a computer. Bunpro, Anki, Satori Reader and the videos all work on a phone, but typed Bunpro answers and the notebook work are easier at a desk.

## What I could not verify

- I have not seen inside the two Shin Kanzen Master N4 books. Their structure is taken from the publisher's pages, and the time I allow per lesson is an estimate.
- The section titles of the introduction to *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar* are from memory. If the book's contents page differs, follow the book.
- Bunpro has a yearly price, but I could only read the monthly and lifetime prices from its pricing page.
