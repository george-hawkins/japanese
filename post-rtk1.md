# After RTK1: what to do with it

You finished RTK1 and now see 撤 as fingers, bring up, taskmaster, but no English word. That is the expected end state, not a gap. The keyword was scaffolding for learning the shape; words replace it.

## What should happen when you see 撤

**In isolation.** Kanji are morphemes, not words, like *-tract* in *retract* and *extract*. Recognising a distinct, known shape that you could write, and that is not 徹, is the correct and complete response right now.

**Inside a word.** The target is that 撤退 is read as てったい and understood as "withdraw" in one step, in about the time it takes to read the English word. Decomposing into parts is the building material for the first few exposures and the fallback when whole-word recall fails. If you are still decomposing a word after weeks of reviews, the word is not learned yet. Parsing is a diagnostic, not a success.

## How much kanji-to-keyword recall do you need?

Three associations can fire when you see a kanji:

1. **Shape.** "I know this one, and it is not 徹." RTK1 built this. It is all a lone kanji needs to trigger.
2. **Keyword.** "Remove." A hook while you are learning a word, not an end in itself.
3. **Words and sound.** "撤退, 撤去, テツ." The real target. Only vocabulary builds it.

The rule for the keyword: never drill it, but have it available cheaply while learning a word. If it surfaces within a few seconds, use it. If not, look it up; jpdb.io shows a keyword on every word page. Your passive kanji-to-keyword recall will improve anyway, because every lookup inside a real word strengthens it. That is harmless: what you keep is the word, not the label. Heisig's objection was to drilling the reverse direction in isolation, which trains a translation reflex.

Once you know two or three words with a kanji, association 3 takes over from 2. You then guess new compounds from "the 撤 of 撤退", a wordless sense of pulling back or taking away, rather than from "remove".

## Where RTK1 pays off

1. **Discrimination.** You will not misread 撤退 as 徹底. When two words do get tangled, name the component that differs and make a one-line split: 扌 removes, 彳 penetrates.

2. **Chunking.** 撤退 is two known units, not two dozen strokes. Spend the memory you saved on the reading and the sentence.

3. **Meaning hook on first exposure.** Compose the keywords into a gloss, then check it against the real meaning. For 撤 it works about half the time:

   | Word | Reading | Keywords | Real meaning |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 撤退 | てったい | remove + retreat | withdraw troops, or pull out of a market |
   | 撤去 | てっきょ | remove + gone | clear away a structure or a parked car |
   | 撤回 | てっかい | remove + times | retract a statement |
   | 撤廃 | てっぱい | remove + abolish | abolish a rule or a tariff |
   | 撤収 | てっしゅう | remove + income | strike camp, pull equipment out |

   "Times" and "income" do nothing for you here. The keyword is a unique label Heisig needed to tell every kanji apart, not a definition. Use it when it helps and drop it when it does not.

   For single-kanji words with okurigana, which make up much of Kaishi, the keyword usually just is the meaning: 持つ hold, 待つ wait, 退く retreat. There all your work is the reading.

4. **Guessing unknown words in the wild.** 撤廃 seen cold reads as "remove + abolish", and the guess is right. 皮肉 as "pelt + meat" meaning sarcasm shows the limit. Treat the guess as a hypothesis and confirm it in a dictionary.

5. **Phonetic components.** RTK1 taught you to see the parts. Now watch which parts predict sound. 撤 and 徹 share 育 + 攵 and both read テツ, so 徹底 is てってい and 徹夜 is てつや. Even 轍 in 轍を踏む, a kanji outside RTK1, reads テツ for the same reason. Each time you learn an on-yomi, ask whether another kanji with the same right side has the same reading. This is the content of RTK2, picked up for free as you go.

6. **Lookup.** You can write any kanji into handwriting input or find it by component, so dictionary lookup is never a blocker.

7. **Writing by hand, if you keep it.** Kaishi is recognition only, so writing decays unless you keep the RTK deck in review-only mode. Keep it with no new cards for now; it is cheap and keeps the keyword hooks warm while you lean on them. Drop it later if handwriting is not a goal.

## What RTK1 does not give you

These are the new targets for every Kaishi card. 撤退 stays as the example even though a beginner deck is unlikely to contain it.

1. **The reading, as a property of the word.** Learn てったい, not "撤 is テツ". After two or three 撤 words the on-yomi is yours anyway. Never study a kanji's reading list from a dictionary. Without the reading you cannot say the word, hear it or type it, so a card is not passed until you have it. Learn the two sound rules explicitly:
   - A first element ending in ツ or チ usually becomes っ before k, s, t or h, and h then becomes p; ク and キ usually do so before k. テツ + タイ gives てったい, テツ + ハイ gives てっぱい, 学 + 校 gives がっこう, but 徹夜 stays てつや.
   - The second element often voices: 手紙 is てがみ.

2. **Kun versus on.** A kanji standing alone or with okurigana usually takes its kun-yomi; a two-kanji compound usually takes on-yomi. 退く is しりぞく and 退院 is たいいん. Kun readings are arbitrary with respect to the shape; the keyword helps with the meaning only.

3. **Actual meaning, collocation and register.** "Remove" does not tell you that 撤回 takes 発言 and 撤去 takes 看板. The example sentence does. A compositional gloss is not knowing the word.

4. **Grammatical class.** 撤退 is a する noun, often with から: 市場から撤退する. For kun verbs you need the transitivity pair, 退く and 退ける. For adjectives, い or な. None of this is in the kanji.

5. **Sound.** Play the audio every time and say the word aloud. RTK graduates are typically strong visual readers and weak listeners, because meaning arrived through the eyes. If the card shows pitch accent, learn it with the word.

6. **Automaticity.** Allow a couple of seconds for reading plus meaning from the whole word. Decompose only on failure, and grade "I got it by decomposing" as hard or again, never good. Start reading real text early, graded readers or NHK News Web Easy. Sight words come from volume, not from reviews.

7. **Irregular words and kana words.** 大人 おとな, 今日 きょう and 風邪 かぜ ignore the kanji readings entirely. Learn them as wholes. A large share of Kaishi is kana-only words where RTK is irrelevant.

8. **Correcting the keyword.** Let words overwrite the label. 生 is "life" in RTK but is student in 生徒, teacher in 先生, draft in 生ビール and raw in 生卵. After enough words the shape carries its own Japanese semantic field and the English label fades, which is the outcome Heisig intended.

## Routine for a new card, using 撤退

1. Confirm the two kanji as known shapes. Note the 徹 lookalike.
2. Recall or look up the keywords, compose a gloss, compare it with the card's meaning.
3. Learn the reading てったい. Note テツ + タイ with っ. Ask whether another kanji shares 育 + 攵: yes, 徹, also テツ.
4. Read the sentence. Note する-noun, takes から, formal register.
5. Play the audio, say it, note pitch if shown.
6. In reviews, demand reading and meaning from the whole word within a couple of seconds. Grade decomposition as hard or again.
