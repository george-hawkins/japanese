Stroke order for 友, 左, 右, 有, 布 and the 厷 of 雄
====================================================

Five jōyō kanji, and one component, all open with the same two strokes, a horizontal 一 and a left-falling ノ, crossed to form the ナ-shaped element that Heisig calls _by one's side_. The rest of the character then sits in the bottom right:

| kanji | keyword | remainder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 左 | left | 工 |
| 友 | friend | 又 |
| 右 | right | 口 |
| 有 | possess | 月 |
| 布 | linen | 巾 |
| 厷 | (see below) | ム |

The odd one out is 厷. It is not a jōyō kanji and has no Heisig keyword of its own, but it is the left half of the jōyō kanji 雄 (male), which Heisig builds as _by one's side_ + _elbow_ (ム) + _turkey_ (隹). Since it has exactly the same layout as the other five, ナ with something tucked in the bottom right, it belongs in the group even though you will never write it on its own.

The catch is that the order of those two strokes is not the same across the group. Half write 一 first, half write ノ first, and the usual rule of thumb ("the stroke that ends up longer is written second") is correct but useless in the moment, because it makes you stop and think. The goal is to make the order automatic, and that means learning the two shapes as two different primitives rather than one primitive with a rule attached.

The split is 3 versus 3
-----------------------

- **一 first: 左, 友 and 厷 (雄).** The 一 is a short stub and the ノ is a long sweep down the left side. Think of it as a *tall* ナ.
- **ノ first: 右, 有 and 布.** The 一 is wide and reaches right across the top of the 口, 月 or 巾 below, and the ノ is short. Think of it as a *wide* ナ, a lid over a box.

Once your eye reads these as two shapes, the stroke order rides along with the shape. You stop asking "which order?" and start writing "the stub one" or "the lid one". The only thing left to memorize is which ones are the stub group, and one sentence covers that:

> **My friend on my left lies flat.**

友 and 左 start with the flat stroke, and so does the male 雄 standing beside them. Everything else in the set sweeps first.

How to drill it so it sticks
----------------------------

Drilling is the right tool for a motor habit like this, but a few tweaks make the difference between a sheet you fill in once and a habit that survives.

1. **Interleave and randomize.** Never write the same character in a block of ten. Blocked practice feels smooth and fades fast. A random sequence forces the retrieval you actually need at review time.
2. **Exaggerate the difference on paper.** Make the 左 stub tiny and its ノ long. Make the 右 bar wide enough to cover the whole 口. Your hand learns the shape, and the shape carries the order.
3. **Say the first stroke aloud** for the first few sessions, "flat" or "sweep", then drop it once it feels silly.
4. **Write compounds, not lone characters:** 左右, 左手, 右手, 友人, 有名, 布団, 英雄. Context gives the motor memory more hooks to hang on.
5. **Keep it short and spaced.** A couple of minutes a day for two or three weeks beats one long sheet. Spacing wins for this kind of learning.

Kanji that contain the group
----------------------------

A number of other jōyō kanji contain one of the six, or a larger component built around it. The good news is that the shape test gives the right answer in every one of them: a stub ナ is written 一 first, a lid ナ is written ノ first, exactly as it is in the standalone character. Stroke orders below are taken from [KanjiVG](https://kanjivg.tagaini.net/).

| kanji | component | ナ written | behaves like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 雄 | 厷 (一 ノ ム) | 一 first | 左, 友 (stub) |
| 援 媛 緩 暖 | 爰 (⺤ 一 友) | 一 first | 友 (stub) |
| 惰 | 左 + 月 | 一 first | 左 (stub) |
| 差 | 羊-top + 左 | 一 first | 左 (stub) |
| 佐 | 左 | 一 first | 左 (stub) |
| 抜 髪 | 友 | 一 first | 友 (stub) |
| 随 堕 髄 | 陏 (ナ 月) | ノ first | 有 (lid) |
| 賄 郁 | 有 | ノ first | 有 (lid) |
| 希 | メ + 布 | ノ first | 布 (lid) |
| 怖 | 布 | ノ first | 布 (lid) |
| 若 諾 匿 | 右 | ノ first | 右 (lid) |

The two non-jōyō components here, 爰 and 陏, are like 厷 in that you only meet them inside bigger characters, but unlike 厷 they contain a complete 友 or 有, so they need no separate rule. (陏 is historically 左 with the 工 dropped, the traditional form of 随 being 隨, but in its modern shape it is simply 有 and is written like 有.)

Near neighbours deliberately left out
-------------------------------------

- **灰** (ashes) opens with the same ナ in its Japanese form, but the Chinese form uses 厂, and the character reads as a different shape.
- **在** and **存** open 一, ノ, then the hook of 才 down the left side, so the left half is heavier than in the six above. KanjiVG decomposes them with 才 rather than ナ.

The 厂 family (石, 反, 后, 厄, 圧, 厚, 原 and relatives) also starts 一 then ノ, but there the ノ drops from the left end of the 一 instead of crossing it, so the confusion never arises.
