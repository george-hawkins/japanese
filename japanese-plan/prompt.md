# Prompt to reproduce these plans

Paste the text below into a new session. It replaces the original long prompt, the six Gemini conversations and the answers I gave to follow-up questions. It asks for both plans and the comparison, which is what the original session ended up producing.

---

Design two alternative study plans for my Japanese, and a comparison of them, and write them as Markdown files in the current directory.

**Goal.** Take me from a restart at JLPT N5 to the point where I can begin the Shin Kanzen Master N3 books. I will not sit the N5 or N4 exams. We will plan N3 separately when I get there.

**Time.** Two hours per day, five or six days a week.

**Where I am now.**

- I completed Heisig's *Remembering the Kanji* volume 1 and still review it daily, so I know the shapes and English keywords of about 2,200 kanji. I know almost no readings and I do not know Chinese.
- I read hiragana and katakana fluently.
- I studied before and half-remember much of N5 grammar and vocabulary, but it is disorganised. I want to start from the beginning and I will not be bored by that.
- I like Anki, and I am happy doing written exercises with a pen.

**Constraints and preferences.**

- My main risk is giving up for lack of structure, not lack of effort. I am not good at imposing my own structure. I want to be told exactly what to do in each session and each week, as a textbook with a workbook would.
- I want normal kanji orthography from the start. I do not want material that writes words in kana because the learner is assumed not to know kanji.
- I am uncomfortable with grammar being forced into English grammatical categories, which is why Cure Dolly's explanations appeal to me.
- I have ruled out Minna no Nihongo as too dry for self-study.
- I see the value of Genki's exercises and workbook, but I have been told that Genki is printed largely in kana. Establish how the third-edition textbook and workbook are actually printed before relying on that. If PDFs of the books are in this directory, examine them directly.
- Include handwriting. Include speaking only as solo practice. I will hire a tutor at the N3 stage.
- Do not include anime or music for now.
- I already subscribe to Natural Japanese (formerly Comprehensible Japanese), Migaku, Skritter and Kanshudo, and I have favourable impressions of Bunpro and of Tokini Andy. Do not include anything because I pay for it or like it, and do not exclude anything because it is thinly covered online. Judge each on its merits. I am willing to buy books and subscriptions and would prefer one or two books too many to missing something useful, for example *The Kanji Code*.

**What to produce.**

- **Plan 1**, built around the combination I currently favour: Cure Dolly's video series, the Kaishi 1.5k Anki deck, Bunpro, *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar*, a beginner listening source, and graded readers (Tadoku or Satori Reader, your choice). Evaluate each part critically, and keep, replace or drop it on its merits.
- **Plan 2**, built around the Genki textbooks and workbooks as the source of grammar order and written exercises, keeping whatever from Plan 1 still earns its place. Find out what a Tokini Andy membership offers and say whether it belongs in this plan.
- For each plan: what to do in each two-hour session, what is new in each week or unit through N5 and N4, objective checkpoints, a list of what to buy and subscribe to and when, and rules for missed days and for reviews that take too long.
- **A comparison** of the two plans with your honest recommendation.

**How to work.**

- Ask me about anything that is unclear instead of assuming.
- Check your time arithmetic. Each plan must fit in two hours a session, and I must not be juggling more resources at once than a person can manage.
- Verify facts about resources against primary sources such as publishers' and the services' own sites. Weigh the quality of any source you find and do not treat the top search results as authoritative. Say what you could not verify.
- Make the plans as long and detailed as they need to be and no longer.
