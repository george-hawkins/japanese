# Sessions

Every session is two hours and consists of the same blocks in the same order. Do the blocks in the order given. A timer for each block helps.

There are three kinds of session. An **A session** introduces new material, and there are five in each unit. A **B session** consolidates the unit, and there is one at the end of each unit. A **T session** is a timed test, and there are three in the whole plan.

## Session layouts

| 5 | Weak points | none | 20 min |
| 6 | Dictionary introduction | none | 20 min |
| 7 | Composition | none | 20 min |

In Units 1 and 2 the two review blocks take much less than their budget, because few items are due yet. The spare time goes to the Cure Dolly videos, of which there are four per unit at the start.

## How to do each block

### Anki

Do your RTK reviews first, then the Kaishi reviews, then the new Kaishi cards.

For a Kaishi card, look at the word, say its reading aloud, and recall its meaning. Then show the answer. Press Good only if both the reading and the meaning were right. Otherwise press Again. Do not use Hard. Press Easy only for a new card whose word you already know without effort, which will happen often in the first two units.

After showing the answer, listen to the sentence audio and repeat the sentence aloud once. This is part of your speaking practice.

Settings, in the deck options for Kaishi:

- FSRS switched on, with desired retention at 90%.
- New cards per day: 10.
- Maximum reviews per day: 9999, so that Anki never hides due cards.
- Learning steps: 1m 10m.
- New cards shown after reviews.

Ten new cards in each of six sessions is 60 words per unit, which finishes the deck at the end of Unit 25. After the first few weeks the Kaishi part of this block should be about 80 to 100 reviews and take 20 to 25 minutes.

From Unit 26 the new cards are words you collect in the Satori Reader block, as described under that block.

### Bunpro reviews

Type each answer. When you get one wrong, read the explanation that Bunpro offers before moving on. After each answer, read the complete sentence aloud.

### New grammar

The schedule gives the Bunpro lesson for each unit. In Phase 1 a lesson is spread over the five A sessions of one unit, which is two to four points per session. In Phase 2 a lesson is spread over the ten A sessions of two units, which is about two points per session.

For each new point:

1. Read the structure box and the explanation.
2. Play the audio of each example sentence and read the sentence aloud.
3. Add the point to your reviews and do the short quiz that Bunpro gives.

Allow about five minutes for a point that you half-remember and up to ten for one that is new.

When the schedule lists a Cure Dolly lesson for the session, watch it before the Bunpro points, with subtitles on. Watch it once without pausing to take notes. Afterwards write no more than five lines in your notebook stating what the lesson claimed. In Phase 1 the lessons come in order, one per A session, starting from the first A session of the unit, so a unit with three lessons has two A sessions without a video.

Many early N5 points will be familiar. Go through them at the same steps but faster. Do not skip them, because the purpose of this pass is to replace your mixed-up memories with one ordered set.

### Notebook

For each grammar point you learned in this session:

1. Copy one of its example sentences by hand, in full kanji and without furigana. Say it aloud as you write.
2. Write one sentence of your own that uses the point and at least one word from your recent Kaishi cards. Mark it with a star.

Your RTK knowledge means you can write the kanji without a stroke-order reference. If you cannot recall how to write a character, look at it, cover it, and write it from memory.

In a session without new grammar points, copy five sentences from Bunpro reviews that you got wrong that day.

### Weak points (B sessions, Phases 1 and 2)

Bunpro keeps statistics on which points you get wrong. Take the three points with the worst record in the unit. For each one, open *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar* at the pages listed in the Resources section of the Bunpro point, read the entry, and copy its key sentences into your notebook by hand. If the point has a Cure Dolly video listed, re-watch it if you have time left in the block.

### Dictionary introduction (B sessions, Phase 1)

Read the section listed in the schedule. The sections are a few pages each. After reading, close the book and write three or four sentences in English in your notebook that state the section's main claims. This short act of recall is what stops the reading from being passive, which was the concern you raised with Gemini.

### Composition (B sessions)

1. Write a short text by hand, in Japanese, about anything: five sentences in Phase 1, growing to about ten by Phase 3. Use at least three grammar points from the current unit. This takes about ten minutes.
2. Type the text and the starred sentences from the unit's A sessions into Claude or a similar assistant, or photograph the notebook page, with this request:

   > I am learning Japanese and am at about JLPT N5 [or N4] level. Below are sentences I wrote. For each sentence, tell me whether it is grammatical. If it is not, give the smallest change that makes it correct and explain the error in one or two sentences. If it is correct but unnatural, say so and give a more natural version that uses only grammar and vocabulary at N4 level or below. Do not rewrite sentences that are already correct and natural.

3. Rewrite each corrected sentence by hand underneath the original.

Typing the text requires the Japanese input method on your Mac, which is worth setting up in any case. Current assistants are reliable at finding grammatical errors at this level. Their weakness is rewriting more than is needed, which the request above is worded to prevent.

### Listening: Natural Japanese

On the Watch page, set the level filter to your current level, set Status to "Unwatched Only" and sort by "Easiest". Watch whatever comes first in that list, so that there is nothing to choose. Watch one or two videos, to fill the block, without subtitles and without pausing. Beginner videos have a median length of nine minutes, but some run to half an hour, and a long one can be split across two sessions. Do not look anything up and do not make flashcards. If you understood less than about two thirds, watch the same video again in the next session before taking a new one.

Start at Complete Beginner. Move up to Beginner when you follow nearly everything in Complete Beginner videos on the first viewing, which I expect around Unit 6. Move to Intermediate by the same test, which I expect somewhere in Units 20–30. If a new level leaves you unable to follow the gist, go back a level for another unit.

### Reading: Tadoku (Phase 1)

Read books at the level given in the schedule. Follow Tadoku's own rules: do not use a dictionary, skip over anything you do not understand, and abandon any book that bores you. Read at least part of each book aloud. When a book has audio, read it once silently and then once more while listening.

Move up a level when you have read the free books at the current level or when you read one of them without stopping. The schedule gives the levels I expect, but the test just stated takes priority over the schedule.

### Reading: Satori Reader (Phases 2 and 3)

In the settings, show all words in kanji and choose to reveal furigana when you tap a word.

Read one episode per session, in this order:

1. Listen to the whole episode once without looking at the text.
2. Read it sentence by sentence. Try to read each word before tapping it for the reading. Read the editors' notes. Look at the translation of a sentence only after you have formed your own understanding of it.
3. Listen to the whole episode again while following the text.
4. Play five sentences one at a time and repeat each aloud immediately after the speaker.

If an episode takes more than 30 minutes, stop at 30 and finish it in the next session.

Do not make flashcards from Satori Reader while Kaishi is still supplying your new cards. From Unit 26, install the Yomitan browser extension, connect it to Anki through AnkiConnect (which you already have), and add up to ten words per session from the episode you are reading. Choose words that Yomitan's frequency information shows to be common. The Kaishi README links to a list of note types made for this purpose. These cards become your new cards in the Anki block. Do not use Satori Reader's own flashcard feature.

### Shin Kanzen Master N4 Grammar (Phase 3)

Work through two items per A session in the order of the book, where an item is one form-practice section, one lesson, or one review test. Write all answers by hand in your notebook, in full sentences where the exercise allows it. Mark them from the answer booklet immediately. Put a mark in the book beside every question you got wrong.

The estimate of 20 minutes per item is mine and I have not seen the book. If two items regularly take more than 40 minutes, do what fits in 40 minutes and let Phase 3 run for an extra unit.

### Corrections (B sessions, Phase 3)

Redo by hand every question you marked wrong in the unit, without looking at your earlier answer. For any that you get wrong a second time, read the entry in *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar* and the Bunpro explanation.

### T sessions

A T session is the test and nothing else. Print the papers, use the times printed on each paper, play the listening audio once without pausing, and mark the test from the answer key afterwards. Convert each of the three parts (vocabulary, grammar with reading, and listening) to a percentage. If you have energy afterwards, do your Anki and Bunpro reviews. Otherwise leave them for the next day.

## Rules

### When reviews take too long

1. Always finish both review blocks, even if they run over their budgets.
2. Always give listening and reading their full minutes.
3. Take the lost time out of the new-grammar and notebook blocks. If there is not enough time left for new grammar points, learn fewer or none in that session. The unit then needs an extra A session, which is acceptable.
4. If the Anki block takes more than 35 minutes in three sessions out of five, set new cards to 5 per day. Return to 10 when the block has been under 30 minutes for a week.

These rules mean that new material is what gives way when you are overloaded. This is the right way round, because reviews that take too long are caused by too much new material.

### Days off and missed days

- On a planned day off, do nothing. If you want to shorten the next session, do only the due Anki reviews and stop when the new cards appear.
- After one or two missed days, continue with the next session in the schedule. The review blocks will be longer than usual and the rules above apply.
- After three or more missed days, do sessions consisting only of Anki reviews without new cards, Bunpro reviews, listening and reading, until both review queues reach zero within a session. Then continue from where you stopped.
- Never reset a deck and never restart the plan from Unit 1.

### Things not to do

- Do not add a resource that is not in the plan before Unit 36. If something looks useful, write it on a list and raise it when the plan is reviewed.
- Do not make your own flashcards for grammar. Bunpro already tests it.
- Do not increase new Kaishi cards above 10 in order to finish sooner. The grammar schedule sets the length of the plan, so finishing the deck early gains nothing and makes every session heavier.

### Tracking

The tables in [schedule.md](schedule.md) have a box for each session. Tick it when the session is done. Write the date and one line about anything that went wrong at the back of your notebook. That record is what we will use when the plan is reviewed.
